Stay Safe This Diwali

Complete Fireworks Safety Guide 2026

Expert safety rules for before, during, and after lighting fireworks — protecting your family all festival season

✅ Pre-Lighting Quick Checklist

  • ✔️ Choose a clear, open area away from trees and buildings
  • ✔️ Keep a large bucket of water and a hose nearby
  • ✔️ Wear well-fitting, 100% cotton clothing
  • ✔️ Wear closed-toe shoes
  • ✔️ Have a dedicated lighter or long ignition stick (agarbatti)
  • ✔️ Assign a sober adult to be in charge
  • ✔️ Ensure children are at a safe viewing distance
  • ✔️ Check wind direction before lighting
  • ✔️ Keep pets indoors in a quiet room
  • ✔️ Have a first aid kit readily accessible

Before You Light

Preparation is the key to a safe celebration.

📍

Safe Location

Choose an open area away from dry grass, trees, vehicles, and buildings. Ensure a flat, stable surface for ground items.

🪣

Water Ready

Always have a reliable water source. A bucket of water, a hose, or a fire extinguisher should be immediately accessible.

👕

Proper Attire

Wear thick cotton clothing. Avoid loose-fitting clothes, dupattas, or synthetics that can catch fire easily.

📦

Organized Storage

Keep unused fireworks in a closed box away from the firing area to prevent accidental ignition from sparks.

📖

Read Instructions

Read the labels on all fireworks before dark. Understand how they operate before attempting to light them.

🚫

Sober Lighting

The person designated to light fireworks must be completely sober. Alcohol and fireworks are a dangerous mix.

While Lighting: 10 Golden Rules

  1. One at a Time: Light only one firework at a time. Never try to light multiple fuses simultaneously.
  2. Clear the Area: Ensure everyone is at a safe distance before igniting.
  3. Never Relight Duds: If a firework fails to go off, wait 20 minutes, then soak it in water. Never try to relight it.
  4. Don't Hold Fireworks: Never hold lit fireworks in your hand, except for specific handheld items like sparklers.
  5. Keep Your Distance: Use a long incense stick (agarbatti) or extended lighter. Don't place any part of your body over the firework.
  6. No Throwing: Never throw or point fireworks at people, animals, buildings, or vehicles.
  7. Avoid Glass/Metal: Never light fireworks inside glass bottles or metal containers to prevent dangerous shrapnel.
  8. Move Quickly: Once the fuse is lit, back away immediately to a safe distance.
  9. Watch the Wind: Be mindful of wind direction so sparks don't blow toward spectators or flammable materials.
  10. Proper Disposal: Soak all spent fireworks in a bucket of water before throwing them in the trash.

Kids Safety Guide by Age

Age Group Safe Fireworks Safety Advice
Under 5 Years None (Spectator Only) Keep at a safe distance indoors or held by an adult. Use ear protection.
5 - 8 Years Sparklers (with extreme caution), Pop-pop snappers Constant adult supervision. Ensure they understand sparklers stay hot after burning out.
8 - 12 Years Ground spinners (Chakkars), Small flower pots Adult must be present. Teach them how to light using a long agarbatti and step back.
12 - 16 Years Fountains, Small aerials (under supervision) Supervise closely. Ensure they understand the rules of not throwing or altering fireworks.
16+ Years Most fireworks Must demonstrate responsibility and follow all safety guidelines. Still requires adult presence.

*Parental discretion is advised. Always follow local laws and regulations regarding fireworks usage.

Emergency First Aid

Minor Burns

  • Cool the burn with running water for 10-15 minutes.
  • Do NOT use ice, as it can damage tissue.
  • Do NOT apply butter, grease, or powders.
  • Cover loosely with a clean, dry cloth.
  • Seek medical help if the burn is large or severe.

Eye Injuries

  • Do NOT rub the eye or attempt to flush it out yourself.
  • Do NOT apply pressure to the eye.
  • Cover the eye gently with a shield (like a paper cup).
  • Seek immediate emergency medical attention.

Hearing & Other Issues

  • For ringing ears, move away from the noise source.
  • If someone inhales excessive smoke, move them to fresh air immediately.
  • In case of a clothing fire, remember: STOP, DROP, and ROLL.

Storage & Disposal Rules

Storing Fireworks Safely

Environment: Keep in a cool, dry place. Moisture ruins fireworks, and heat can be dangerous.

Location: Store away from living areas, ideally in a locked shed or garage, out of reach of children.

Separation: Keep away from flammable liquids, electrical panels, and direct sunlight.

Proper Disposal

Duds: Wait 20 minutes, then soak completely in a bucket of water overnight.

Spent Fireworks: Soak in water before placing them in the regular trash to prevent latent embers from starting a fire.

Never: Burn unused fireworks in a bonfire or try to dismantle them.

Safety FAQ

Store fireworks in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight, moisture, and any sources of heat or ignition. Keep them out of reach of children and pets, ideally in a locked cabinet.
Always wear close-fitting, 100% cotton clothing. Avoid synthetic fabrics like nylon or polyester, which can easily catch fire and melt onto the skin. Wear closed-toe shoes.
If a firework fails to ignite (a 'dud'), do not attempt to relight it. Wait at least 15-20 minutes, then approach it carefully and douse it thoroughly with water before disposing of it.
Spectators should maintain a safe distance of at least 30 feet for ground-based fountains and sparklers, and 75-100 feet or more for aerial fireworks like rockets and multi-shots.
For a minor burn, immediately run cool (not ice-cold) water over the affected area for 10-15 minutes. Do not apply ice, butter, or ointments immediately. Seek medical attention if the burn is severe or covers a large area.
